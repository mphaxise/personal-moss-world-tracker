## Editorial frame

Working chapter title:
- `Bernal Heights Moss Atlas`

Working walk title:
- `Post-Rain Bernal Loop`

Editorial promise:
- hidden fog-fed texture of Bernal Heights
- walls, bark, exposed slopes, stairs, and wet soil microhabitats
- a neighborhood-scale walk that makes overlooked cryptogam life feel legible

Internal biological scope:
- mosses
- liverworts
- lichens

Public framing:
- moss atlas

## Walk shape

Working walk parameters:
- duration: 60 to 90 minutes
- distance: approximately 2.0 to 2.5 miles
- terrain: hilly sidewalks, stairs, and park paths
- season emphasis: post-rain, but usable year-round
- audience: curious walkers, neighborhood explorers, and design-minded urban naturalists

Narrative arc:
1. start with built surfaces where moss is easiest to recognize
2. move into stairs and retaining walls where moisture patterns become legible
3. climb toward exposed Bernal edges where lichen and fog exposure become part of the story
4. finish with street trees, gardens, and neighborhood texture that connects back to daily life

## Stop template requirements

Each stop must ship with:
- working title
- public-safe location hint
- coordinates or explicit approximate marker choice
- one hero image
- one short summary
- one `why this spot matters` block
- one habitat label
- one seasonality note
- one species note, even if broad
- at least one source or field note reference

Optional but useful:
- second detail texture image
- nearby comparison note
- `what to look for` prompt for walkers

## Field verification checklist

A candidate stop becomes `field-verified` only if it passes all of these checks:
- visually distinct enough to justify its own stop
- safe and publicly accessible
- story is place-based, not generic taxonomy filler
- image opportunity is strong enough for the atlas format
- habitat claim can be described honestly without overclaiming species certainty

A candidate stop should be dropped if:
- it depends on private property access
- the growth is too weak or inconsistent to photograph clearly
- the story duplicates a stronger stop nearby
- the site is unsafe to pause at

## Editorial guardrails

Write stops like a neighborhood guide, not a taxonomy database.

Good stop writing should answer:
- what is happening here
- why here instead of somewhere nearby
- what should a walker notice
- what this reveals about Bernal as a place

Avoid:
- overclaiming species certainty from a photo alone
- generic moss trivia that could apply anywhere
- turning every stop into the same `green thing on wall` description
